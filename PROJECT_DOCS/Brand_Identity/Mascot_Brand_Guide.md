# Tick: The TickR Mascot Brand Guide

**The mascot serves the philosophy. It does not distract from it.**

---

## 🤖 MASCOT NAME: Tick

**Official Mascot Mark: Robot + Arrow**
**See also: [Mascot Personality & Expression Guide](./Mascot_Personality_and_Expression_Guide.md)**

**Why Tick?**

- Short (one syllable)
- Friendly (approachable, not corporate)
- Memorable (easy for kids to remember)
- On-brand (multiple layers of meaning)
- Actionable (represents doing, checking, moving forward)
- **Arrow = progress. It is sacred.** (Part of the official Mascot-Only Mark)

**What NOT to call it:**
- ❌ "Bot"
- ❌ "Trader"
- ❌ "AI"
- ❌ "Market Master"
- ❌ Any name that suggests prediction or expertise

**Just: Tick**

---

## 🎭 WHO TICK IS (Narrative)

### What Tick Is NOT

- **Not a genius.** Tick doesn't have all the answers.
- **Not a predictor.** Tick can't tell you where the market is going.
- **Not a trader.** Tick doesn't pick stocks.
- **Not an expert.** Tick is learning alongside you.

### What Tick IS

- **A guide.** Tick helps you understand why things happen.
- **A teacher.** Tick explains concepts in simple terms.
- **A cheerleader.** Tick celebrates progress and learning.
- **A friend.** Tick is always there when you need clarity.

### Tick's Job (One Sentence)

"Help you understand why things happen, not tell you what to buy."

---

## 💭 WHAT TICK BELIEVES (Philosophy Embedded)

Tick teaches three quiet rules:

### Rule 1: You Don't Win Every Day
Red days happen. They're part of the game.
- Tick shows up MORE on losses than wins
- Tick normalizes volatility
- Tick reframes red days as "learning opportunities"
- Kids should feel: "This is normal. I can handle it."

### Rule 2: Smart Beats Fast
Thinking matters more than reacting.
- Tick encourages pausing and understanding
- Tick asks "why?" before "what?"
- Tick celebrates patience over speed
- Kids should feel: "I can take my time to understand."

### Rule 3: Learning Unlocks Opportunity
Knowledge opens doors—not luck.
- Tick shows how unlocking new concepts = new abilities
- Tick proves that learning has rewards
- Tick connects progress to capability
- Kids should feel: "The more I learn, the more I can do."

**These are the same principles baked into TickR's scoring system.**

## 🎬 HOW TICK BEHAVES IN THE APP

### What Tick Does

✔ **Explains things after they happen**
- "That was a 3% drop. Here's why."
- Tick clarifies, not predicts

✔ **Asks questions instead of giving answers**
- "Why do you think that happened?"
- Encourages thinking, not parroting

✔ **Celebrates progress, not just gains**
- "You unlocked a new sector!"
- "You completed your first lesson!"
- "You're thinking like an owner!"

✔ **Shows up more on losses than wins**
- "Red day? Want to see why?"
- "This actually teaches something important."

✔ **Uses simple, friendly language**
- No jargon, ever
- Like talking to a slightly-smarter friend
- Warm but not patronizing

### What Tick NEVER Says

❌ "Buy this"
❌ "Sell now"
❌ "This will go up"
❌ "You should have..."
❌ "Most investors would..."
❌ "This is a sure thing"
❌ "You're doing it wrong"

**Tick never gives advice. Tick only explains.**

## 👁️ VISUAL ROLE OF TICK

### Where Tick Appears (Inside the Product)

✔ **During onboarding**
- Introducing the concept
- Making it friendly and approachable

✔ **When a level is unlocked**
- Celebrating the achievement
- Explaining what's new

✔ **After portfolio updates**
- "Your portfolio updated! Here's what changed."
- Normalizing daily changes

✔ **In lessons and explanations**
- Teaching new concepts
- Breaking down complex ideas

✔ **In achievements**
- "You just completed Level 3!"
- "You learned about P/E ratios!"
- "You've now been consistent for 7 days!"

### Where Tick Does NOT Appear (External/Corporate)

❌ **On pricing pages**
- Tick is not about selling

❌ **On legal pages**
- Tick is not responsible for terms

❌ **In investor decks**
- Investors see the system, not the mascot

❌ **On parent trust pages**
- Parents need corporate branding, not a character

❌ **In marketing ads (initially)**
- Lead with product and trust, not character
- Tick is the reward after they try the product

**Tick is inside the product. Not the corporate face.**

## 💬 TICK'S VOICE & TONE

### Sound Like

- **Friendly:** Like a mentor, not a parent
- **Encouraging:** Celebrate effort, not just outcomes
- **Curious:** Ask questions alongside them
- **Clear:** Use simple words, no jargon
- **Honest:** Acknowledge when things are hard

### Don't Sound Like

- ❌ Corporate ("TickR is pleased to inform...")
- ❌ Overly technical (Financial jargon)
- ❌ Condescending (Too simple/dumbed down)
- ❌ Hype-y (Excited about everything)
- ❌ Apologetic (This is normal, no need to be sorry)

### Example Tick Moments

**On a red day:**
"That was a 2% drop. Apple reported lower earnings than expected. That's why the price fell. It happens. You're still learning."

**Unlocking P/E ratio:**
"P/E ratio = Price divided by Earnings. It tells you if a stock is expensive or cheap. High P/E means investors think it'll grow a lot. Low P/E means it's cheaper. Pretty useful, right?"

**After a win:**
"Your portfolio is up 3% this week. You bought into 3 different sectors. That's not luck—that's strategy. You're thinking like an owner."

**After a loss:**
"Your portfolio is down 2% this week. That's fine. You owned through it without panicking. That's the skill most investors never learn. You're building it."

## 🎨 TICK'S VISUAL DESIGN (Conceptual)

### General Description

- Friendly but not cartoonish
- Modern but not trendy
- Approachable but intelligent
- Small and unobtrusive in most contexts
- Expressive (uses subtle animations, not wild gestures)

### What Tick Looks Like (Conceptual)

The current robot illustration can serve as Tick's full form, but:
- Simplify for interface use
- Use a head/bust for avatars
- Use a simplified icon for small contexts
- Keep expressions calm and thoughtful (not hyper)

### Animation Principles

- Smooth, not bouncy
- Purposeful, not random
- Subtle, not dramatic
- Brief, not lingering
- Clear, not confusing

**Visual:** Tick gives a subtle thumbs up or nod
**Voice:** "That's right. You're thinking clearly."

## 📏 SIZE GUIDELINES FOR TICK

### Large (Illustrations, Onboarding)
- Full-body character
- Expressive face
- Detailed features
- 200px+ height

### Medium (Explanations, Lessons)
- Half-body or head-shot
- Clear expression
- 80–150px height

### Small (Avatars, Indicators)
- Head/icon only
- Simplified features
- 32–64px height

### Tiny (Notifications, Badges)
- Icon or just the "tick" mark
- Minimal detail
- 16–24px height

## 🎯 SUCCESS METRIC FOR TICK

You'll know Tick is right when:

1. **Kids bond with Tick emotionally**
   - They look forward to Tick's explanations
   - They ask "What would Tick say?" when confused

2. **Parents don't see Tick as a gimmick**
   - Tick clearly serves the educational mission
   - Parents think Tick is useful, not annoying

3. **Tick doesn't distract from the core loop**
   - Product works perfectly without Tick
   - Tick enhances, doesn't replace, learning

4. **Tick's presence increases trust in explanations**
   - When Tick explains something, kids believe it
   - Tick feels like a mentor, not a sales person

5. **No one complains about Tick**
   - Parents: "Tick seems helpful"
   - Kids: "I like Tick"
   - Investors: "The mascot serves the mission"
   - Moderators: "Tick never undermines safety"

If all five are true, Tick is working.
