# TickR Brand Presentation by Audience

**Which version to show to who. This matters more than people think.**

---

## 👨‍👩‍👧 PARENTS (Decision Makers)

### What to Show

✔ **Clean TickR wordmark**
- Professional, bold, trustworthy
- No fluff, no character

✔ **Minimal icon (optional)**
- Simple upward checkmark or progress mark
- Reinforces learning/growth
- Understated, not playful

✔ **Calm color palette**
- Deep teal or slate blue
- Soft green accent (only in success moments)
- Clean, professional presentation

### What to Avoid

❌ **Neon or trendy colors**
- Makes it look like a game, not education

❌ **Mascot front-and-center on first impression**
- Wait until they're inside the product
- Let mascot be a surprise, not a sales tactic

❌ **Hype-driven imagery**
- Rockets, arrows, explosive growth
- Anything that signals "gambling"

❌ **Childish design elements**
- Comic-sans fonts
- Over-designed characters
- Anything cutesy

### What They Need to Feel

**"This is safe, intentional, and serious."**

Not:
- "This is fun for kids"
- "This is trendy"
- "This will make my kid rich"

But:
- "This teaches real skills"
- "This is professionally designed"
- "This company thought about safety"

### Parent Touchpoints

| Touchpoint | Show What |
|-----------|-----------|
| Landing page hero | Clean logo + headline |
| Parents page | Logo + trust messaging (no mascot) |
| App store listing | Logo + app icon |
| Email communications | Logo at top (professional) |
| About page | Logo + team photo |
| Legal / terms | Logo + clean, clear text |
| Parent dashboard | Logo + calm UI (no character) |

### Parent Copy Tone

- Professional but warm
- Clear, no jargon
- Evidence-based ("Here's what your child learned")
- Never hype-y ("Your kid is crushing it!")

## 💼 INVESTORS (Capital & Credibility Decision)

### What to Show

✔ **Clean system logo ONLY**
- Professional, scalable, timeless
- Communicates: "This company is serious"

✔ **Maybe a small mascot reference**
- Only on a product/design slide
- "Here's the experience inside the app"
- Not central to the pitch

✔ **Corporate-grade presentation**
- Brand book format
- Technical specifications
- Scalability documentation

### What to Avoid

❌ **Leading with the mascot**
- Investors are evaluating systems, not characters
- Mascot is a detail, not the headline

❌ **Cute, playful branding**
- Makes it look like a toy product
- Undermines seriousness

❌ **Unclear scaling rules**
- Investors want to see rigor
- Show every color mode, every size

❌ **Sloppy logo use**
- Investors notice attention to detail
- Every logo appearance should be perfect

### What They Need to See

**"This team is serious about design and execution."**

Not:
- "This is a fun app for kids"
- "This will be the next Roblox"

But:
- "This is a thoughtfully designed system"
- "Every detail serves the product philosophy"
- "This company has discipline"

### Investor Touchpoints

| Touchpoint | Show What |
|-----------|-----------|
| Pitch deck title slide | Clean logo |
| Company logo examples | Logo at various scales |
| Design system slide | Full branding spec |
| Product demo | Mascot + UI (inside app) |
| Team/culture slide | Logo only |
| Financial materials | Logo only |
| Data room | Complete brand guidelines |

### Investor Copy Tone

- Precise and clear
- Data-driven
- System-focused
- No fluff, no stories (unless relevant)

## 🧒 KIDS / USERS (Engagement & Loyalty Decision)

### What to Show

✔ **Tick the mascot (prominently)**
- Character, personality, expressiveness
- Makes the app feel like "theirs"

✔ **Animations and interactions**
- Tick moves, reacts, celebrates
- Brings the character to life

✔ **Personality in every interaction**
- Tick's voice in explanations
- Tick's presence in achievements

✔ **Progress visuals**
- Unlock animations featuring Tick
- Level-up celebrations with Tick
- Tick in learning moments

### What to Avoid

❌ **Corporate branding**
- Kids don't care about wordmarks
- Don't lead with "TickR" without context

❌ **Overly aggressive character**
- No "hype man" energy
- No constant celebration

❌ **Confusing or scary design**
- Tick should feel safe, not intense
- Simple features, clear expression

❌ **Character that talks down**
- Never patronizing
- Never treats kids like babies

### What They Need to Feel

**"Tick is helping me understand things."**

Not:
- "This app is designed by adults"
- "Tick is trying to sell me something"

But:
- "Tick is my guide"
- "Tick makes it fun to learn"
- "I can trust Tick's explanations"

### Kid Touchpoints

| Touchpoint | Show What |
|-----------|-----------|
| App opening | Tick greeting |
| Onboarding | Tick introducing concepts |
| Portfolio screen | Tick in corner, helpful |
| Lesson screens | Tick teaching |
| Quiz results | Tick celebrating/explaining |
| Achievements | Tick prominent in unlock |
| Red day explanations | Tick being supportive |
| Daily check-in | Tick presence |

### Kid Copy Tone

- Friendly and encouraging
- Clear and simple (no jargon)
- Never patronizing
- Honest about challenges

## 📱 APP STORE ICON

### The Challenge

Your app icon is often the FIRST thing potential users see.

### What to Use

✔ **Simplified Tick head or tick-mark icon**
- Not the full TickR wordmark
- Not the full character
- Just a clear, recognizable symbol

✔ **Single color or two-color max**
- App icons need to be simple
- Detailed characters don't scale to 180px

✔ **High contrast**
- Must be readable on all backgrounds
- Works in dark mode and light mode

### What NOT to Do

❌ **Don't use complex character art**
- Doesn't scale to icon size
- Too detailed for 180px

❌ **Don't use neon or trendy colors**
- Looks like a gaming app or crypto
- Undermines educational positioning

❌ **Don't use the full TickR wordmark**
- Too small to read as icon
- Use as app name in store, not icon

### The Red Flag Test

**If it looks like crypto on the App Store, redo it.**

Apps that fail this test:
- Robinhood (too hype-y for financial education)
- Crypto wallets (neon, futuristic, untrusty)
- Get-rich-quick apps (arrows, explosions, glitz)

Your app icon should feel:
- Calm and professional
- Simple and clear
- Educational, not speculative

### App Icon Specification

- **Format:** 1024 x 1024px (upload), scales down to 180px
- **Color:** Teal/slate blue primary with white accent
- **Style:** Icon only (no text)
- **Detail level:** Medium (not too simple, not too complex)
- **Margins:** Safe area required (don't put design right to edge)
- **Contrast:** Works on white, black, and color backgrounds

## 🎨 LANDING PAGE (The Balance)

### Hero Section (First Impression)

**Show:** Clean TickR logo + headline

- Logo at top-left or center
- Professional, bold, trustworthy
- No mascot (not yet)
- First impression: "This is serious education"

### Value Proposition Section

**Show:** Benefits + mascot teaser

- Still mostly text and icons
- Maybe a small Tick illustration (optional)
- Focus on what parents/kids will learn
- Not on Tick's personality

### Product Demo / Screenshots

**Show:** UI with Tick inside

- First time parents and investors see Tick
- In context of the product
- Clearly serving the educational mission
- Feels natural, not forced

### Messaging in CTAs

**Button text:** "See How It Works" (not "Meet Tick")

- Don't sell the mascot, sell the outcome
- Tick is discovered, not marketed

## 🎯 THE GOLDEN RULE

**Build trust first. Introduce Tick later.**

1. **First Impression:** Parents/investors see professional logo
2. **Second Impression:** They learn about the product
3. **Third Impression:** They see Tick inside the app
4. **Ongoing:** Tick becomes their trusted guide

This order matters. Flip it and you're a game company, not an educational platform.

## ✅ AUDIENCE CHECKLIST

### For Parents Seeing the Brand
- [ ] Feels trustworthy (not hyped)
- [ ] Feels educational (not entertainment)
- [ ] Feels intentional (not like a startup)
- [ ] Logo is professional and clean
- [ ] No forced character personality

### For Investors Seeing the Brand
- [ ] Logo communicates discipline
- [ ] Branding shows attention to detail
- [ ] System is scalable and documented
- [ ] Mascot serves the product, not the marketing
- [ ] Everything feels strategic

### For Kids Using the Brand
- [ ] Logo feels modern (not babyish)
- [ ] Mascot is helpful (not annoying)
- [ ] Mascot shows up when needed
- [ ] Tick is a friend, not a sales person
- [ ] Learning feels fun, not forced

If all three audiences are satisfied, your branding is working.
