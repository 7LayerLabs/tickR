# Fundraising Narrative & Seed Deck Storyline

**The story arc your deck should follow. Not slide design—logic.**

This is the backbone of your pitch. Get this right, and the deck designs itself.

---

## THE NARRATIVE ARC (10-SLIDE STRUCTURE)

### Slide 1: THE PROBLEM (Behavioral, Not Technical)

**Headline:**
"Most people learn investing by losing real money."

**Why this works:**
- Specific, not generic
- Relatable to parents
- Sets urgency
- Leads into solution

**What NOT to say:**
❌ "Financial literacy is broken"
❌ "EdTech has low engagement"
❌ "Gen Z doesn't understand money"

**Speaker notes:**
"The average person's first investment experience is around age 22. By then, they have no framework for thinking about risk, diversification, or emotional discipline. So they lose money. And then they learn."

**Slide visual:**
Not needed. Just text. Clean.

---

### Slide 2: WHY THIS PROBLEM IS GETTING WORSE

**What's changed:**
- Retail access exploding (Robinhood, TikTok trading)
- Younger exposure (hype culture, social media)
- Zero formal education in most schools
- Higher stakes (losing college savings or more)

**Why now matters:**
Sets the urgency. If the problem was always there, why raise now?

**Speaker notes:**
"We've democratized market access without democratizing market literacy. Kids can buy stocks at 12 on a parent's phone. But no one is teaching them how to think about them. TikTok is."

**Slide visual:**
Chart: Retail trading growth + Gen Z involvement + Zero financial literacy in schools

---

### Slide 3: WHY EXISTING SOLUTIONS FAIL

**Frame it as "The Gap":**

| Solution | Strength | Weakness |
|----------|----------|----------|
| EdTech (Khan) | Content | No engagement, kids leave |
| Stock games (MarketWatch) | Fun | Encourages gambling, parents hate |
| Brokerages | Real | Too risky, won't serve minors |
| Finance TikTok | Hype | Teaches terrible habits |

**Speaker notes:**
"Edtech is boring and kills retention. Games encourage reckless behavior. Brokerages won't touch minors. And TikTok? It's hype dressed as education."

**Slide visual:**
2x2 matrix: Engagement vs. Educational Value
- Edtech: High education, low engagement
- Stock games: High engagement, low education
- TickR (to be shown next): High education, high engagement

**Key message:**
"No one owns the middle."

---

### Slide 4: THE INSIGHT

**Headline:**
"You can't teach investing without real markets—but you can remove real money."

**This is the thesis.**

**Speaker notes:**
"The insight is this: Kids need to experience real market behavior—real price moves, real earnings reports, real sector rotation. But the real money part? That just adds risk without adding learning.

TickR uses real stocks at real prices. But the money is simulated. That's the lever that makes this work."

**Slide visual:**
Simple: Real Market + Play Money = ?

### Slide 5: THE PRODUCT (System, Not Screens)

**DON'T SHOW UI. Show the system.**

**What to highlight:**
- Real stocks, real prices
- Simulated cash
- Composite scoring (not returns-only)
- Progression-based competition
- Parent oversight

**Speaker notes:**
"Here's how TickR works:

Kids start with play money and buy real stocks. Their portfolio changes with the real market. But winning isn't just about returns. Our scoring rewards diversification, consistency, and learning progress. So gambling behavior can't win.

Competition is private-first. Global tournaments are earned through learning levels.

Parents have full visibility. They see what concepts their kid has learned, what risk behaviors are forming."

**Slide visual:**
Flow diagram:
```
Kid Signup
→ Buy Real Stocks (play money)
→ Portfolio Updates Daily
→ Learn Why Prices Move
→ Compete (if they want)
→ Unlock New Sectors/Metrics
→ Progress from Level 1→7
```

### Slide 6: WHY THIS IS DEFENSIBLE

**Headline:**
"Our moat is system design, not data."

**What's defensible:**
- Age-appropriate progression (unlock metrics)
- Scoring that punishes gambling
- Safe community that parents tolerate
- Game loop that doesn't teach day-trading

**Speaker notes:**
"Anyone can show stock prices. Few can do:
- Progressive gating where you can't cheat progression
- Composite scoring that's exploit-resistant
- A community that's safe but not boring
- A game loop that teaches discipline instead of addiction

That design choice is what we're protecting. That's the moat."

**Slide visual:**
Compare TickR vs. competitors:
- Returns-only leaderboards (competitor)
- Composite scoring (TickR)

Show visually how YOLO strategy loses in composite scoring.

**What to show (depending on stage):**

**Slide visual:**
- Retention curve (D1, D3, D7, D14, D30)
- Activation rate (% buying stock within 10 min)
- Conversion rate (free to paid)

### Slide 8: BUSINESS MODEL

**Keep it simple. Don't overcomplicate.**

**Revenue:**
- Free tier: Starter features
- Paid tier: $5.99/month or $49/year
- Target conversion: 12–15% within 30 days

**Unit economics (conservative):**
```
10,000 users
× 12% conversion
= 1,200 paid users
× $5.99/month
= $7,188/month
= ~$86k/year
```

**Expansion paths:**
- Family plans (2–4 kids)
- Schools ($50–500 per school/year)
- Curriculum packs
- Affiliate revenue (future, parents only)

**Speaker notes:**
"We're a SaaS company. The model is simple: free tier hooks kids, paid tier unlocks value. We're intentionally not building complex monetization—we're proving subscription works first.

At scale, we have multiple paths: families, schools, tournaments. But the core is subscription."

**Slide visual:**
Simple breakdown:
- Revenue model (subscription)
- Conversion targets
- Expansion paths (show 3 options)

### Slide 9: MARKET & SCALE

**Don't over-TAM this. Be precise.**

**Market size:**
- 60M Gen Z kids in US
- 70% interested in investing
- 5–10% willing to use disciplined platforms
- TAM: 2–6M kids

**Expansion to schools:**
- 30k US schools teaching economics
- 130k after-school programs
- Market: $50M–500M (licensing model)

**Geographic expansion:**
- English-speaking countries first (5x TAM)
- Then international (10x+ TAM)

**Speaker notes:**
"We're not saying TickR will capture billions. We're saying there's a real market of millions of kids and parents willing to pay for structured financial training. And schools are already trying to solve this problem—they just don't have a good tool."

**Slide visual:**
Waterfall chart:
```
US Gen Z ages 12–16: 60M
Interested in investing: 42M
Addressable (paid users): 5–10M
Conservative Y1 capture: 10k users (0.1–0.2%)
```

## HOW TO PRACTICE THIS NARRATIVE

### Solo
- Write out speaker notes for each slide (not full sentences, just key points)
- Practice delivering in 10 minutes (2 slides per minute)
- Record yourself, then watch

### With a Friend
- Have them interrupt with questions
- Don't read slides; speak naturally
- Adapt narrative based on their questions

### In Front of Investors
- Know your first 2 slides cold
- Know your business model perfectly
- Be ready for "Weak Spot" questions on any slide
- Have data ready for questions

---

## CRITICAL RULES

### DO:
- ✔ Tell a story (problem → solution → why now → why you)
- ✔ Show evidence (beta users, retention, conversion)
- ✔ Be honest about risks (and show mitigation)
- ✔ Own your weakness (know what investors will worry about)

### DON'T:
- ❌ Show 20 slides (max 12)
- ❌ Use industry jargon (keep it simple)
- ❌ Make up metrics (use real data or say "estimated")
- ❌ Oversell the market (be precise, not grandiose)
- ❌ Hide the business model (they're paying for revenue, not heartstrings)

## THE NARRATIVE IN ONE SENTENCE

"Kids learn investing backwards—with real money first, then consequences. TickR flips that, using real markets but play money, with structured progression that teaches discipline instead of gambling."

If you can say that clearly, you've got the narrative.
