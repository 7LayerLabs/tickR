# TickR - Learning Curriculum

## Educational Philosophy

### Core Principles

1. **Learn by Doing** - Every concept connects to trading activities
2. **Real-World Relevance** - Use companies and examples teens know
3. **Bite-Sized Content** - 5-minute lessons max
4. **Immediate Application** - Quiz → Trade → Reinforce
5. **Kid-Friendly Language** - No jargon without explanation

### Content Framework

Each lesson follows the **DESK** structure:
- **D**efinition - What is it?
- **E**xample - Real-world analogy
- **S**teps - How it works
- **K**ey Takeaway - Main point to remember

## Path 1: Stock Market Basics

### Overview
- **Theme**: Foundation knowledge
- **Goal**: Understand what stocks are and how markets work
- **Prerequisites**: None (starter path)
- **Total XP**: 250

### Lesson 1.1: What is a Stock?

**Duration**: 3 min | **XP**: 50

**Content:**
```
DEFINITION:
A stock is a tiny piece of ownership in a company. When you
buy a stock, you become a part-owner of that business!

EXAMPLE:
Imagine if you could buy a piece of Apple. Every time Apple
sells an iPhone or MacBook, a tiny bit of that money is
technically yours as a shareholder.

HOW IT WORKS:
1. Companies split themselves into millions of pieces (shares)
2. They sell these shares to raise money
3. You can buy and sell these shares
4. The price changes based on how well the company does

KEY TAKEAWAY:
When you buy a stock, you're not gambling - you're buying
a real piece of a real business.
```

**Quiz:**
```
Q: What do you get when you buy a stock?
A) A loan to the company
B) A piece of ownership ✓
C) A guaranteed profit
D) A gift card

Explanation: Stocks represent ownership! When you buy Apple
stock, you literally own a tiny piece of the whole company.
```

### Lesson 1.2: How Stock Prices Work

**Duration**: 4 min | **XP**: 50

**Content:**
```
DEFINITION:
Stock prices are determined by supply and demand. If more
people want to buy than sell, the price goes up!

EXAMPLE:
Think of concert tickets for your favorite artist. If everyone
wants tickets but there aren't many, prices go up. If nobody
wants them, prices drop. Stocks work the same way.

HOW IT WORKS:
1. Buyers and sellers meet on the stock market
2. If more buyers → price rises
3. If more sellers → price falls
4. Prices change constantly during trading hours

KEY TAKEAWAY:
Stock prices reflect what people THINK a company is worth,
not always what it's actually worth.
```

### Lesson 1.3: The Stock Market

**Duration**: 4 min | **XP**: 50

**Content:**
```
DEFINITION:
The stock market is a place where people buy and sell stocks.
It's like an online marketplace, but for company ownership!

EXAMPLE:
Think of it like eBay for companies. Instead of selling old
video games, people are buying and selling pieces of real
businesses like Netflix, Nike, and Tesla.

HOW IT WORKS:
1. NYSE and NASDAQ are the two main US markets
2. Markets open at 9:30 AM and close at 4:00 PM Eastern
3. Millions of trades happen every second
4. Electronic systems match buyers with sellers

KEY TAKEAWAY:
The stock market is just a fancy matching service that
connects people who want to buy with people who want to sell.
```

### Lesson 1.4: Reading Stock Quotes

**Duration**: 5 min | **XP**: 50

**Content:**
```
DEFINITION:
A stock quote shows you the current price and other key
information about a stock. It's like a stock's report card!

EXAMPLE:
AAPL (Apple) $185.50 +$2.30 (+1.25%)
This tells you Apple stock costs $185.50, up $2.30 today,
which is a 1.25% increase.

HOW IT WORKS:
- Ticker Symbol: Short code (AAPL = Apple)
- Current Price: What it costs right now
- Change: How much it moved today
- Percent Change: The change as a percentage
- Volume: How many shares traded

KEY TAKEAWAY:
Learning to read stock quotes is like learning a new
language - once you know it, you can understand any stock!
```

### Lesson 1.5: Buying Your First Stock

**Duration**: 5 min | **XP**: 50

**Content:**
```
DEFINITION:
Buying a stock means exchanging your money for shares of
a company. You're now a part-owner!

EXAMPLE:
If Netflix costs $400 per share and you have $1,000, you
could buy 2 shares and have $200 left over.

HOW IT WORKS:
1. Choose a stock you want to own
2. Decide how many shares to buy
3. Check if you have enough cash
4. Place your order
5. The shares appear in your portfolio!

KEY TAKEAWAY:
Start small, learn as you go, and remember: you only need
ONE share to be a real investor!
```

## Quiz Design

### Question Types

**Multiple Choice (Primary)**
- 4 options
- 1 correct answer
- Explanation after submission

**Example:**
```
Q: A company with a low P/E ratio might be:
A) Always a good investment
B) Undervalued OR having problems ✓
C) About to go bankrupt
D) A technology company

Explanation: Low P/E can mean the stock is a bargain,
or it might mean investors are worried about the company.
Always investigate why!
```

### Pass Requirements
- **Pass**: ≥70% (3/4 questions)
- **Perfect**: 100% (4/4 questions)

### XP Rewards
- Pass: +75 XP
- Perfect: +150 XP

## Content Guidelines

### Language Rules

**Do:**
- Use simple words
- Give real examples
- Connect to familiar brands
- Use analogies
- Be encouraging

**Don't:**
- Use unexplained jargon
- Assume prior knowledge
- Make it feel like school
- Overwhelm with details
- Be condescending

### Example Transformations

| Complex | Kid-Friendly |
|---------|--------------|
| "Market capitalization represents the total value of a company's outstanding shares" | "Market cap is like a price tag for the whole company" |
| "The P/E ratio indicates how much investors pay per dollar of earnings" | "P/E shows if a stock is expensive or cheap compared to how much money the company makes" |
| "Diversification reduces unsystematic risk" | "Don't put all your eggs in one basket" |
